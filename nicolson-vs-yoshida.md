Context:
- Nicolson's two recent KOs came against low-level opposition (Singh, Campos). Her real level is UD wins over Mahfoud, Vargas, Chapman, Luna and Turner, with clear scorecards.
- All five of Yoshida's losses are decisions. She has never been stopped, and she has also never stopped anyone of note.
- Yoshida had not fought since April 2025, a layoff of about 18 months. The BoxingInsider/FIGHTMAG dates for the Flores fight conflict (Apr 2025 vs Apr 2026). Wikipedia's table says 2025.

## Style Matchup

- **Range and footwork:** Nicolson is an Olympic-style southpaw. She uses a wide stance, lateral movement and counter-punching, and wins by out-landing opponents at range. Yoshida is a high-work-rate, come-forward volume puncher with little power, whose game is built at 115–118.
- **Who imposes the fight:** Nicolson. She has a height and reach edge of about 2 inches plus a natural-size advantage, since she campaigned at 126. Yoshida has to walk in through the southpaw's lead hand without the power to make Nicolson respect her on the way in.
- **Stance:** Southpaw against orthodox favours Nicolson's straight left to the body and head as Yoshida steps in.
- **Power:** Neither fighter projects a stoppage threat at this level. That means a points fight, which suits the cleaner, more accurate boxer.
- **The real danger for Nicolson:** Her only loss came to Tiara Brown, a strong pressure fighter who made the fight messy and won a split decision on activity. Yoshida's style is the closest thing on her schedule to Brown's, but Yoshida is smaller, older and less physically imposing.

## Physical & Current Factors

- Age gap of 7 years, with Yoshida at 38 and coming off a long layoff.
- Yoshida is moving up to a third weight class. Her best work was at 115–118, and she lost twice to the bigger Metcalf at 118.
- Nicolson had an injury that cost her the August date. Nothing reported suggests it carried over, and she made weight comfortably.
- The fight is on US soil with neutral judging. Nicolson has won wide cards in the US before.

## Key Evidence

- **Metcalf beat Yoshida twice by clear UD.** Metcalf is a bigger, stronger boxer, and that is the most relevant sample for Yoshida facing a natural-size disadvantage.
- **Yoshida beat Ebanie Bridges (UD).** It shows she can out-work a slower, hittable champion. Nicolson is neither slow nor hittable.
- **Nicolson's loss to Brown (SD).** The blueprint against her works, but it needs physicality and pressure Yoshida has not shown at 122.
- **Nicolson's 100-90 type wins over Mahfoud and Vargas.** They show she can neutralise game but limited opponents for 10 rounds.

## Path to Victory — Nicolson

- **Primary:** Keep the fight at range with the jab and straight left, pivot off the ropes, and win clean-punching rounds on the scorecards.
- **Secondary:** Counter Yoshida's entries and accumulate body work. A late stoppage is possible if Yoshida fades, but unlikely given Yoshida's durability.
- **Failure point:** Getting dragged into exchanges along the ropes and letting Yoshida's work rate steal close rounds, as happened against Brown.

## Path to Victory — Yoshida

- **Primary:** Relentless forward pressure and volume. Turn rounds into close, busy rounds, cut the ring off and work inside where reach matters less.
- **Conditions:** She needs to sustain a pace at 38 after a long layoff and at a new weight, and judges have to reward activity over accuracy.
- **Failure point:** If she cannot close distance without eating counters, she loses round after round. She lacks the power to force a change.

## Prediction

**Nicolson by unanimous decision.** Estimated win probability is about **85–90%** for Nicolson, and the most likely shape is wide scorecards (98-92 territory). A stoppage is unlikely: Yoshida has never been stopped, and Nicolson is not a puncher at this level. Yoshida's upset path is a Brown-style volume fight that tips close rounds her way.

The partial live data (Nicolson 4-0 on the blogger's cards and about 2:1 on CompuBox) is consistent with this read but unofficial.

## Confidence / Main Uncertainty

**High confidence.** The main uncertainties:
- whether Yoshida's pressure can recreate Brown's success
- how Nicolson's injury layoff and Yoshida's 18-month inactivity actually showed up on the night
- the conflicting record counts at BoxingNews, plus the fact that Tapology and BoxRec could not be checked directly because of the bot check
