# The Schoharie Limousine Crash (October 6, 2018)

**What happened:** A stretch limousine carrying a birthday party lost its brakes on a long, steep hill in Schoharie, New York. It went through a stop sign at more than 100 mph, hit a parked SUV, and ended up in a ravine. All 18 people in the limo died, and so did 2 people standing in a parking lot. With 20 dead, it was the deadliest transportation accident in the U.S. since the 2009 Colgan Air crash near Buffalo.

**Why it happened, in one sentence:** The operator sent out a vehicle that the state had taken off the road over safety problems, with brakes that had rusted badly, driven by a driver who was also barred from carrying passengers. Weak state oversight let that operator keep running for years.

## 2. The day of the crash

- **The group:** 17 friends and relatives, ages 24 to 34, were going from Amsterdam, NY, to Brewery Ommegang near Cooperstown for **Amy Steenburg's birthday** (reports say she was turning 30). Among them were four sisters and several married couples, some only recently married.
- **Warning signs during the ride:** Passengers texted family that the limo was a "junker" in "terrible condition" with an unusually loud engine. According to Wikipedia, one text a few minutes later said the brakes were burning and the car was coasting. The limo left Amsterdam late and took a roundabout route: the Thruway, then NY-30A, then NY-7, then NY-30. Nobody knows why it went that way.
- **The driver:** Scott Lisinicchia, 53, was a former truck driver who held a CDL. He left his home in Lake George around 10 a.m. to get the limo in Saratoga Springs. He was not listed as a driver on the limo's insurance. Another Prestige driver had reportedly refused to drive the Excursion for 18 months because its brakes were bad. The NTSB could not tell whether Lisinicchia did a pre-trip inspection. A proper one should have shown the ABS warning light on and a front tire that did not match the others.

### The crash sequence (NTSB)

1. The limo turned right from NY-7 onto **NY-30** and started down a **1.81-mile hill** to a T-intersection with **NY-30A**, which has a stop sign. The road drops about **573 feet**, with grades up to **11.35%**. The speed limit is 55 mph, and a 50 mph advisory sign stands 914 ft before the stop sign.
2. Shortly after the turn, witnesses saw the limo move onto the shoulder with its **hazard lights on**. They said the driver looked "confused" and "frazzled." It then pulled back into the lane and **picked up speed** going downhill. Investigators could not determine why it pulled over.
3. The driver probably pressed the brakes, but they **did not slow the vehicle**. Its speed rose above 100 mph.
4. At the bottom, a Jeep was stopped at the stop sign. Its occupants said the limo sounded like a **"jet engine."** The driver swerved left across the double yellow line, missed the Jeep, and went **through the stop sign and across both lanes of NY-30A**.
5. The limo went into the driveway of the **Apple Barrel Country Store** and hit an empty **Toyota Highlander** at an estimated **101–118 mph** (median 109 mph). The Highlander was thrown forward and hit **Brian Hough (46) and his father-in-law James Schnurr (70)**, who were standing next to it.
6. The limo, still going about 80 mph, went about 55 more feet into a **ravine** and hit an embankment and trees. It ended up in the streambed.
7. All 20 victims died of **blunt-force trauma**. Emergency crews got there 4 minutes after the 911 call.

## 3. Why the brakes failed

The NTSB examined the vehicle after the crash. That was only a visual inspection, because the DA and State Police would not allow anything more for a long time. Here is what it found:

- **Rear brakes:** A **steel brake line was badly rusted and crimped** close to the right-rear hose, which would have cut off fluid to that brake. Oil and brake fluid on the line pointed to a **leak**. The **right-rear caliper was heavily corroded and both pistons appeared seized**. One left-rear piston appeared stuck as well. The left-rear pads looked almost unused, which suggests that brake had not been getting full pressure for a long time.
- **Front brakes:** Signs of **overheating**: a burned smell, pad material embedded in the rotors, and glazing. With the rear brakes doing little, the front brakes took most of the load on the hill and **faded**.
- **Weight:** The limo weighed about **10,000 lb empty and about 13,565 lb with 18 people**. That is roughly **4,965 lb over** the SUV's original 8,600 lb rating. Ford's rules for stretched Excursions called for an upgraded chassis kit, including a Hydro-Boost brake booster, and a cap of 9,900 lb.
- **But maintenance was the deciding factor:** The NTSB had a lab load an Excursion to the limo's crash weight and simulate this exact hill on a brake dynamometer. **Brakes in proper condition would have stopped it safely at the bottom.** The over-limit weight and lack of certification were real problems, but the immediate cause was **brakes nobody had maintained**.
- **Ruled out:** Weather, road signs, sight distance, alcohol, fatigue, cell-phone use, and the driver's experience with the vehicle. Marijuana and prescription drugs were found in the driver's system, but the NTSB **could not say whether they impaired him**. He had not disclosed his marijuana use in his CDL medical exam; had he done so, he would not have been medically certified to drive.

## 4. Who is to blame: the NTSB's probable cause

> "…Prestige Limousine and Chauffeur Service's **egregious disregard for safety**, in dispatching a stretch limousine with an out-of-service order for a passenger charter trip, resulting in the failure of its brake system while descending the steep grade of New York State Route 30. Contributing to the crash was the **New York State Department of Transportation's ineffective oversight** of Prestige Limousine, despite its knowledge of the carrier's multiple out-of-service violations and lack of operating authority, as well as the department's inadequate repair verification process. Further contributing to the crash was the **New York State Department of Motor Vehicles' inadequate oversight of state-licensed inspection stations and its failure to properly register the limousine**, which enabled Prestige Limousine to circumvent the state's safety regulations and more rigorous inspection requirements."
> — NTSB/HAR-20/03

### How the safety system broke down, layer by layer

| Safeguard | How it failed |
|---|---|
| **Vehicle alterer** (21st Century Coachworks) | No evidence it did the federal testing or added the required second certification label after stretching the vehicle. |
| **Operator** (Prestige / Nauman Hussain) | Ran without state operating authority from 2016 until the crash. Kept understating seating capacity to avoid bus inspections. Had no real maintenance program. Sent out a vehicle and a driver that were **both** under out-of-service orders. |
| **Driver** | Had no passenger endorsement and was under an OOS order. Left drug use off his medical form. May have skipped the pre-trip inspection. |
| **Inspection stations** (Wilton Truck Center, Mavis Discount Tire) | **Knowingly** inspected and passed an obviously altered stretch limo, which DMV policy banned. Mavis's former manager later told investigators that brake work billed to Prestige was not actually done. Mavis denies doing anything wrong. |
| **NYS DMV** | Did not check the registration paperwork, so a vehicle long known as an 18-seat bus was registered as an 8–11-seat car. Did not police inspection stations. Used a different definition of "bus" than NYSDOT. |
| **NYS DOT** | Knew Prestige was operating illegally and had OOS violations, but had no process to verify repairs and no stronger penalties for repeat offenders. An inspector saw the limo parked at Mavis in June 2017, and the NOVs still did not stop it. |
| **Occupant protection** | Most rear seats had lap belts only, many were **out of reach** or not designed for crashes, and no passenger was wearing one. Crash forces were far beyond what anyone could survive. |

### Disputes over the investigation
The NTSB said the **Schoharie County District Attorney (Susan Mallery) and the New York State Police** kept it from getting timely access to the vehicle and evidence because of the criminal case. Prestige would not hand over maintenance records. Because of this, the NTSB could not take the brakes apart or test the brake fluid, and it relied on the dynamometer simulation instead. The NTSB said these obstacles delayed the investigation but did not make it less accurate.

## 5. The people

- **Nauman Hussain** ran Prestige day to day. He was charged in October 2018.
- **Shahed Hussain**, his father, was listed as the company's owner. He was a well-known **FBI informant**: he was convicted of fraud in 2002 and then worked as an informant, including in the 2009 "Newburgh Four" terrorism sting. He was in **Pakistan** when the crash happened and did not return.
- **Victims:** 17 passengers, the driver Scott Lisinicchia, and the two men in the parking lot, Brian Hough and James Schnurr.

---

## 6. Criminal case: *People v. Hussain*

- **Charges:** 20 counts each of second-degree manslaughter and criminally negligent homicide.
- **Arrest and indictment:** Hussain was arrested on Oct 10, 2018, and released on $150,000 bail. In April 2019 he was indicted on 20 counts of each charge. COVID then pushed the trial back repeatedly.
- **Plea deal:** In September 2021 Hussain pleaded guilty to 20 counts of criminally negligent homicide. The deal called for **5 years' probation, 1,000 hours of community service, and a lifetime ban from the transportation industry**. Many of the families were outraged. In 2022 Judge **Peter Lynch**, who took over after Judge George Bartlett retired, **refused to approve the deal**. He pointed to the removed out-of-service sticker as proof the violation was deliberate, called the agreement "completely disingenuous," and said he would accept a plea only with prison time of **1⅓ to 4 years**. Hussain withdrew his plea and went to trial. He later argued he had only 15 to 20 minutes to make that decision.
- **Trial (May 8–17, 2023):** Prosecutors said Hussain knew the limo had been taken off the road and sent it out anyway. DOT inspector Chad Smith, who had put out-of-service stickers on the limo twice, testified. The defense blamed Mavis, saying Hussain had paid for brake repairs and believed they were done. The prosecution's expert said the master cylinder Mavis replaced was working, and that the brake failure came from other problems a careful owner would have noticed. Other testimony said a Mavis manager had once told Hussain to **"burn" the limo** rather than keep fixing it.
- **Verdict (May 17, 2023):** Guilty of 20 counts of second-degree manslaughter, one for each victim.
- **Sentence (May 31, 2023):** **5 to 15 years** on each count, served at the same time. The earliest parole eligibility reported is about May 2028.
- **Appeal:** Hussain argued he was given only about **20 minutes** to decide whether to take the judge's new plea offer and that the jury instructions were wrong. On **Nov 7, 2024**, the Appellate Division, Third Department, **unanimously upheld** the conviction. It found the state had proved Hussain "was aware of and consciously disregarded" the poor condition of the brakes. It agreed the short decision window was a mistake but said it did not change the outcome. It also said the sentence "reflects the grievous harm caused by his recklessness and his callousness." Reports say New York's highest court, the Court of Appeals, declined to take the case in 2025. As of early 2025 Hussain was at Attica Correctional Facility.
- **Mavis:** **No one at Mavis was charged with a crime.** A DMV administrative judge fined the Saratoga Springs store **$9,000** for the inspection but did not revoke its license; that was not made public until 2023. The Saratoga County DA asked State Police for more information, but no charges followed.

---

## 7. Civil cases

Families sued Hussain and his father, **Mavis Discount Tire**, and the State of New York, among others. The suits say Mavis did not do the brake work it charged for and used a vise-grip on a brake hose, which Mavis denies. Mavis settled with **18 of the 20 families around the 2023 trial** on confidential terms. The **driver's estate** settled later. Reporting in 2026 counted 19 of 20 families settled with Mavis. No court ever ruled on whether Mavis was liable.

## 8. What changed afterward

**Immediate state action (late 2018 to early 2019):** Stretch-limo inspections were taken away from regular inspection stations. Vehicles built for more than 9 passengers plus a driver were reclassified as buses. In January 2019, State Police seized the plates of 59 limos. Governor Cuomo proposed banning stretch limos outright but dropped the idea after the industry lobbied against it.

**April 2019 state budget:** Operating a limo in a way that causes a death became a **felony**. Civil penalties and required insurance went up, police got clearer power to **seize plates** from limos with unrepaired OOS violations (Assembly Bill A.9056), inspection stations must report when a vehicle that belongs in the DOT program tries to get a regular inspection, and stretch limos may no longer make **U-turns**. The U-turn ban responds to the 2015 Cutchogue crash.

**Later limo-safety package (passed June 2019, signed early 2020):**
- Stretch limos must have **seat belts** for every passenger. Existing limos had until **January 2023** to be refitted, and vehicles converted from 2021 onward needed them from the start.
- Limo drivers carrying 9 or more passengers need a **CDL with a passenger endorsement**.
- Tougher rules to keep **defective vehicles** off the road.

**Industry effect:** According to an industry group, by April 2022 about 60% of New York's stretch-limo operators had gone out of business.

**Insurance:** Prestige's insurer, Global Liberty, capped total coverage at **$500,000 for all 20 victims**. The insurer was later put into liquidation.

**NTSB recommendations** (HAR-20/03 and the 2019 occupant-protection report):
- **FMCSA:** Give states guidance on enforcing out-of-service orders (H-20-24).
- **State of New York:** Carry out the 2014 State Comptroller audit's recommendations on verifying repairs and stepping up enforcement (H-20-25). Make DMV use DOT's definition of a bus, meaning 10 or more seats (H-20-26).
- **NYS DMV:** Tighten controls and remind stations that they may not inspect stretch limos with 10 or more seats (H-20-27, -28).
- **National Limousine Association:** Tell operators to get an engineer's certification and confirm the secondary FMVSS label before buying an altered vehicle (H-20-29).
- **NHTSA:** Require **lap/shoulder belts** at every seat in new limousine conversions, plus minimum seat-strength standards (H-19-14, -15). At the time of the final report, the NTSB rated NHTSA's response "Open—Unacceptable."

---

## 9. Main lessons

1. **Out-of-service orders only work if someone enforces them.** Putting a sticker on a vehicle and mailing a letter did nothing to stop an operator willing to ignore both.
2. **Gaps between agencies can be exploited.** DMV and DOT defined "bus" differently, so simply putting a smaller seat count on a form moved this limo out of twice-a-year bus inspections and into once-a-year car inspections at a tire shop.
3. **Stretched vehicles fall between the rules.** Each one is a one-off design, and no one checked whether this altered SUV still met the safety standards it was certified to in 2000.
4. **Passengers had no way to check.** NTSB Chairman Sumwalt noted that the customer who booked that morning had no easy way to learn about the operator's OOS orders, and he called for a public, consumer-facing safety database.
