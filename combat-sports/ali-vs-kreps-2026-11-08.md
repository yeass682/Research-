**Ali's opposition:** She is the more tested of the two. Her losses came against her two best opponents:
- **KO 8 to Julissa Guzman (Jun 2023):** stopped in the 8th round. She won the rematch by UD in Nov 2023, which shows she can make adjustments.
- **UD to Yamileth Mercado (Jun 2024), for the WBC 122 title (98-92, 98-93, 97-93):** reports say Mercado "outworked the more accurate Ali." That loss is the clearest guide to this fight.
- **Since then:** wins over Lila Furtado (UD 8) and Singh (KO 1), neither world level.

**Kreps' opposition:** She is unbeaten but has not been tested much as a pro.
- **Known wins:** Brittany Sims (2021), Melissa Odessa Parker (her Marine Corps teammate, described as a step up), Danielle Bennett (TKO, Apr 2025), and Basualdo (SD).
- **Her latest fight was close:** she needed a split decision against a 13-6 Argentine journeyman. Kreps said afterwards that she "made the adjustments."
- **Ali is a step up:** Ali is the best and biggest opponent she has faced.

**What the records hide:** Kreps' 11-0 looks better than Ali's 11-2, but Ali's losses came at a higher level than any of Kreps' wins. Kreps' amateur background (a world medal) is stronger than her pro opposition suggests. Ali's two most recent wins tell us little.

## Style Matchup

**What the fight is about:** Kreps, the shorter and busier fighter, trying to outwork the taller, more correct boxer. This is very close to the Mercado fight that Ali lost.

- **Distance:** Ali has about four inches of height, and almost certainly the longer reach. Her best fight is at range behind the jab and right hand, upright with her guard high and her shots "textbook." Kreps has to cover that distance, either by working her way in behind feints and her jab or by forcing exchanges and making Ali back up.
- **Work rate against accuracy:** Ali has historically been the more *accurate* fighter but not the *busier* one. With 2-minute rounds and judges who reward activity, a fighter who is seen to be working more can steal rounds she loses on clean punches. Mercado did exactly that to Ali. Kreps' amateur style (high work rate, three-round sprints) fits that approach.
- **Ali's defence when stationary:** Ali is upright and can stand still. Against Guzman, staying in range got her stopped. If Kreps can pin her on the ropes, Ali's lack of movement becomes a problem.
- **Body work:** It cuts both ways. Ali's height leaves her midsection exposed to a shorter fighter coming in. But Ali can also dig to the body of an opponent moving forward, and did so on the night (see the live notes below).
- **Power:** Both have 3 KOs in 11+ fights. Neither is a big puncher, so a stoppage most likely needs damage to build up, or a cut.
- **Size:** Kreps is moving up from 118 and Ali is a natural 122-pounder. Ali should be the physically stronger fighter in clinches and exchanges, which works against Kreps' pressure.
- **Age and late rounds:** Ali is 37 and Kreps 30. Kreps should hold her pace better in rounds 7–10; Ali's best chance is building an early lead behind the jab.

**Who is more likely to get the fight she wants?** Probably Kreps. Ali's jab has not stopped busy pressure fighters before (Mercado, Guzman in the first fight). Ali's size is the one thing that could change that.

## Physical & Current Factors

- **Inactivity:** Kreps has been out about 13 months, after a close fight. Ali fought 9 months ago, but against a weak opponent who lasted one round, so neither fighter has had a recent hard test.
- **Size:** Ali is clearly bigger. Kreps' weight (120.8) suggests she did not need a hard cut, and may simply be the smaller fighter on the night.
- **Age:** Ali, at 37, is past the age where reflexes and recovery usually start to fade.
- **Setting:** A neutral US venue. Kreps is American and Ali is British-Somali, but neither has home advantage.

## Key Evidence (most comparable first)

1. **Ali L UD Mercado (2024):** The best guide. A busier opponent beat Ali on points despite Ali's accuracy. That is Kreps' plan.
2. **Ali L KO 8 then W UD Guzman (2023):** Ali can be broken down by a fighter who gets inside, but she adjusted and won the rematch. This shows both a durability concern and the ability to adapt.
3. **Kreps W SD Basualdo (2025):** Kreps' most recent fight was close against a journeyman. Either she wasn't sharp, or she has trouble against awkward opponents. Ali is a much bigger test.
4. **Kreps' 2019 World Championships bronze:** Evidence that her skill level is higher than her pro opposition shows.

## Path to Victory — Ramla Ali

- **Main route:** Win at range with the jab and right hand, using her height. Turn Kreps when she comes in, tie her up when she gets close, and dig to the body as she enters. Bank early rounds while she is fresher.
- **Second route:** Use her size to wear Kreps down in clinches and exchanges. A late stoppage is possible if Kreps' move up from 118 shows.
- **What has to happen:** Ali has to keep moving (not stand still on the ropes) and throw enough to win close rounds, not just land the occasional clean shot.
- **What could go wrong:** Being outworked, as against Mercado, or her defence breaking under sustained pressure, as against Guzman.

## Path to Victory — Mikiah Kreps

- **Main route:** Get past the jab, work the body, make Ali fight going backwards and **out-throw her** in every round. Win on work rate. UD/MD.
- **Second route:** A late stoppage once pressure and body work wear Ali down, or a cut getting worse.
- **What has to happen:** She has to close the distance without taking straight right hands on the way in, and cope with Ali's strength in clinches.
- **What could go wrong:** Being kept at range and losing rounds clearly to the jab, or rust and the step up in size slowing her down as in the Basualdo fight.

## Prediction

- **Most likely winner:** Kreps, about **60–65%**.
- **Most likely method:** Kreps by decision (UD or MD), probably with close scorecards. A stoppage either way is the less likely outcome (about 15–20%). A late Kreps stoppage is more likely than an Ali one.
- **How Ali pulls the upset:** Her height and jab control the distance, her body work punishes Kreps on the way in, and her size wins the clinches. Ali by close decision is a realistic result (35–40%).
- **Betting market:** Kreps was priced at about 70%; I gave that some weight, but my call rests on the matchup.

## Confidence / Main Uncertainty

**Confidence: low to medium.** This is the less clear fight of the two title bouts on this card.
- Kreps' pro opposition is thin and her full record could not be verified (Tapology and BoxRec blocked; no Wikipedia table).
- Kreps' 13 months out and the close Basualdo fight make her current level hard to judge.
- Ali's size edge is the factor most likely to make this pick wrong.

## Result: Ali by split decision (pick wrong)

- **Ramla Ali def. Mikiah Kreps by split decision: 97-92 and 96-93 for Ali, 98-91 for Kreps.** Ali wins the vacant IBF and WBO super-bantamweight titles and is now 12-2 (3 KO); Kreps is 11-1 (Fightmag, Bleacher Report, Yahoo).
- **Knockdown:** Ali dropped Kreps in round 3.
- **After that, Kreps' plan worked as expected:** she was the busier fighter in the second half. Yahoo's unofficial scoring had Kreps ahead from round 6. Bleacher Report scored it 97-92 *Kreps* (8 rounds to 2) and called two of the judges' cards "dubious." Kreps out-landed Ali 91–70 in power punches. Analyst Mikaela Mayer: "Kreps put on enough pressure in the second half of the fight… It deserves a rematch."
- **Review of my breakdown:** I got the shape of the fight right: Kreps outworked Ali in the second half, as Mercado did. I underrated Ali's power and size (the round-3 knockdown) and the judges rewarding Ali's cleaner, harder shots. Most outside scorers had Kreps winning, so the Kreps pick looks right on the action but wrong on the official result. A rematch looks likely to be discussed.

## Live notes (in progress, not official)

From the Yahoo/Uncrowned live blog, using its own scoring:
- **Round 1:** 10-9 Kreps. *"Kreps the busier fighter… as Ali looks to keep it textbook, upright, and with her guard up."* This is Kreps' plan working.
- **Round 2:** 19-19. *"Better round for Ali as she becomes less of a stationary target. Good work from the body, too."* But **Ali was cut** and treated by her cutman. The blog didn't say where the cut is or what caused it.

The early rounds are going much as the breakdown expects: Kreps winning on work rate, and Ali doing better once she moves and goes to the body. The cut adds a risk for Ali.
