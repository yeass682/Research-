**Nicolson's opposition:** Her best wins are Sarah Mahfoud (UD, won the WBC featherweight title), Raven Chapman (UD), Dyana Vargas (UD) and Yulihan Luna (UD, interim 122 title). Her only loss was a split decision to Tiara Brown at 126 lb in Sydney (March 2025). Brown is a strong, athletic pressure fighter who hits hard. Since that loss Nicolson has won four in a row (2 TKO, 2 UD).

**Yoshida's opposition:** She is a three-time world champion: WBO 115 twice, then IBF 118 with a UD over Ebanie Bridges in December 2023. All five of her losses are close or by decision: Koseki (UD 4, early career), Okuda (technical decision after a head-clash cut), Ozawa (SD), and Shurretta Metcalf twice (UD). Since losing to Metcalf she has had two low-level wins (Dudek, de Jesus Flores). **Her 1 KO in 24 fights means she has almost no stopping power.**

**Record notes:**
- Her record has more losses, but those losses are close fights, not a sign she is a weak opponent.
- One preview headline calls Nicolson "undefeated". That is wrong: she is 16-1.
- Sources give different Wikipedia dates for one early Yoshida fight (the Koseki loss). This does not change her record.

## Style Matchup

- **Distance:** Nicolson is a southpaw who moves well and boxes from range, keeping fights at the end of her jab and straight left. She is 4–5 cm taller and probably has a clearly longer reach. Yoshida has to walk forward through that jab to get to work.
- **Pressure without power:** Yoshida's way to win is steady, high-effort pressure that makes the fight messy (her kickboxing background shows in her aggression and toughness). But with **1 KO in 24 fights** she gives Nicolson nothing to be afraid of when she gets inside. The one fighter who has beaten Nicolson (Tiara Brown) beat her with pressure *plus* power and strength at a heavier weight. Yoshida has the pressure but not the rest.
- **Size:** Yoshida is moving up from 118 lb, where she was not dominant (lost twice to Metcalf), to fight a former 126-lb champion. At 122 she is the smaller fighter, so her pressure costs her more and does less.
- **Southpaw vs orthodox:** Nicolson's lead foot outside and straight left against Yoshida's square, forward-moving entries is a well-known way for a southpaw to beat an orthodox fighter.
- **Body work and late rounds:** Yoshida is tough and goes the distance, so a stoppage was unlikely either way. Her best chance would be making Nicolson tired in rounds 7–10. That depends on Nicolson's fitness after the injury and on Yoshida's own sharpness after 18 months out.

## Physical & Current Factors

- **Age and inactivity:** Yoshida is 38 and had not fought for about 18 months, against modest opposition. This is the biggest strike against her.
- **Nicolson's injury:** She withdrew in August, but the fight was then delayed by about 10 weeks and she made weight easily (121.4). Still, it is a real unknown.
- **Weight:** Nicolson, the bigger fighter, cut down to 122. Yoshida is moving up to it. Both made weight comfortably.
- **Venue:** A neutral US venue. Neither fighter had home advantage.

## Key Evidence (most comparable fights first)

1. **Nicolson SD-L Tiara Brown (2025):** This is the template for beating Nicolson, but Yoshida cannot repeat Brown's power and strength. It shows Nicolson can be pressured; it does not show Yoshida can do it.
2. **Nicolson UD Raven Chapman / Mahfoud / Vargas:** Nicolson beat world-level fighters coming forward by boxing well at range, and did it over 10 rounds.
3. **Yoshida L UD ×2 Shurretta Metcalf:** Yoshida lost twice, cleanly, to a fighter with better technique and skill at range at 118. Nicolson is at least as skilled as Metcalf and bigger.
4. **Yoshida UD Ebanie Bridges:** Her best win, but Bridges is a limited fighter who likes to trade at close range. That says little about how she would do against a mobile southpaw like Nicolson.

## Path to Victory — Nicolson

- **Main route:** Keep the fight at range with the jab and straight left, step off to the side as Yoshida comes forward, and win clean rounds on the cards. **UD.**
- **Second route:** Stoppage from accumulated damage late if Yoshida's age and inactivity show (unlikely, since Yoshida is tough).
- **What could go wrong:** Rust from the injury or tiring late, letting Yoshida turn the second half into close-range exchanges.

## Path to Victory — Yoshida

- **Main route:** Constant high-volume pressure, body work, cutting off the ring, and making rounds close and messy so the judges have to choose. **UD/SD.**
- **What has to happen:** Nicolson has to be slow or hurt from her injury, and Yoshida has to be as sharp as she was at her best despite 18 months out.
- **What could go wrong:** She has no power to make Nicolson stop moving, so if she cannot get inside, she loses rounds one after another.

## Assessment (formed before checking the result)

- **Most likely winner:** Nicolson, roughly **80–85%**.
- **Most likely method:** Nicolson by unanimous decision with clear (not close) scorecards. A stoppage either way was unlikely.
- **How Yoshida could pull the upset:** Nicolson's injury showing, plus Yoshida's pressure winning enough close rounds.
- **Betting market:** Champinon priced Nicolson at about 88%. A second "model" listing that favoured Yoshida (59%) disagrees with every other piece of evidence, so I gave it no weight.

## Result: fight over, Nicolson won

- **Nicolson def. Yoshida by unanimous decision: 98-92, 100-90, 99-91** (Fightmag results page). Nicolson keeps the WBC super-bantamweight title.
- **Confirmed by a second source:** Bleacher Report's live blog has a "Fight End" post (9:10 PM) and a "Fight Result" post (9:14 PM). It says: *"Nicholson was a runaway winner on the official cards, taking 27 of a possible 30 rounds from the judges."* That fits Fightmag's cards exactly: 8 + 10 + 9 = 27 rounds.
- **Bleacher Report's own scoring:** 98-92 Nicolson, 8 rounds to 2. Running tally: 30-27 after round 3, 59-55 after round 6, 88-83 after round 9. Round 10: Nicolson *"used her athleticism to keep the cruder Yoshida at arm's length."*
- **Punch stats (Bleacher Report):** Nicolson landed 80 power punches to Yoshida's 50, with 40% accuracy to Yoshida's 28%. The Yahoo/Uncrowned blog, citing CompuBox, said Nicolson had landed about twice as many punches overall by the middle rounds.
- **Analyst comment:** Corey Erdman: *"This wasn't just Nicholson eluding Yoshida… This was Nicholson hitting Yoshida more than anyone's ever hit her."*
- **No knockdowns, cuts or point deductions** were reported anywhere.
- **New records (worked out from the result):** Nicolson **17-1 (3 KO)**, Yoshida **19-6 (1 KO)**.
- **Not yet available:** judges' names and an official result sheet. The MVP results page still read "Coming up" when last checked, and Tapology and BoxRec could not be read.
- **Injury:** One search summary says the August postponement was caused by a **wrist injury**. I could not confirm this from a page I read.

The reported result matches the analysis above: a wide unanimous decision, with Nicolson's range and size beating Yoshida's pressure, which carried no power.

## Main uncertainties

1. Tapology and BoxRec could not be read directly, so the records rest on Wikipedia, the WBC and the promoter.
2. The official scorecards come from Fightmag, and Bleacher Report's "27 of 30 rounds" matches them. The judges' names and the official result sheet are not yet published.
3. No source I could read named Nicolson's August injury.
