Horsepower history

Same engine, different numbers

Automakers retune one engine for each vehicle it goes into: changing the software, turbo boost, intake and exhaust. Pick an engine to see every vehicle and year range it was rated for, or pick a year to compare everything on sale that year.

US-market SAE net horsepower and lb-ft of torque from the makers' published ratings. Diesels sold only outside the US (and BYD) use international ratings converted from kW and Nm. In engine names, "turbo" means one turbocharger and "twin-turbo" means two; engines without either word are naturally aspirated or supercharged (as named). Years with the same rating are grouped. "now" means still on sale for 2026. Dashed bars show an earlier engine, included so you can compare. Hybrid ratings are combined system output; EV ratings are combined motor output, with usable battery size and EPA range where published. ✓ means the rating matched a published source (manufacturer press material, or Edmunds, Car and Driver, MotorTrend, KBB and similar) when re-checked in November 2026. Ratings without a ✓ come from my reference data and have not yet been individually re-checked. Treat them as likely but unconfirmed. "Sources differ" means reputable sources disagree.

Status as of mid-2026, compiled from public reporting and company announcements. The engines and models listed are the major current ones, not every trim or market. Stakes are approximate. Engine codes follow each maker's own naming.
