# Business and Corporate Context of City of Ember (2008)

Source-dating note: I label each source as **[contemporaneous]** (2006–2009) or **[retrospective]** (later, including Wikipedia and box-office databases). Variety, Hollywood Reporter, Screen Daily, LA Times and Kidscreen pages were paywalled or blocked to the fetch tool (HTTP 402/403/405). For those I used search-result snippets or later secondary summaries, and flag where I did. A Bash/curl workaround for Screen Daily was denied by the permission system, so I did not use it.

## 1. Walden Media: ownership, strategy and 2007–2008 track record

### Takeaway
Walden Media was a family/literary-adaptation company founded in 2000, with Philip Anschutz's group as majority owner from late 2001. By 2008 its record was very uneven: the Disney Narnia films were big hits, but the Fox-Walden titles (The Seeker, Mr. Magorium, Nim's Island, City of Ember) mostly disappointed. Its biggest 2008 hit, Journey to the Center of the Earth, was a Warner Bros./New Line release, not a Fox-Walden one.

### Cited Findings
- Walden Media was founded in 2000 by Micheal Flaherty (from education) and Cary Granat (ex-president of Dimension Films). Its formation was officially announced May 7, 2001 (citing Screen Daily, Goodridge, May 7, 2001). Anschutz Entertainment Group bought a majority stake in late 2001, leaving the founders as minority shareholders (citing Variety, Ross & Lyons, Dec. 6, 2001; LA Times, Eller, Nov. 14, 2008) — [Wikipedia: Walden Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walden_Media) [retrospective, citing contemporaneous trades]
- Anschutz is quoted saying he expects the films "to be entertaining, but also to be life affirming and to carry a moral message" (citing Media Transparency, Dec. 2, 2005) — [Wikipedia: Walden Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walden_Media) [retrospective]
- Walden films and distributors before Fox-Walden: Holes (Apr. 2003, Disney); The Lion, the Witch and the Wardrobe (Dec. 2005, Buena Vista); Charlotte's Web (Dec. 2006, Paramount); Bridge to Terabithia (Feb. 2007, Buena Vista, with Summit Entertainment). Other 2008 Walden films: Prince Caspian (May 16, 2008, Disney) and Journey to the Center of the Earth (July 11, 2008, Warner Bros.) — [Wikipedia: Walden Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walden_Media) [retrospective]
- Walden was founded "seven years" earlier to make films based largely on classic children's books. Of its roughly 20 films, only six had grossed over $50M. Walden's Fox films "have mostly disappointed," which the article links partly to scheduling, such as moving Prince Caspian to summer 2008 — [Christianity Today, Peter T. Chattaway, c. mid-Oct 2008](https://www.christianitytoday.com/2006/10/walden) [contemporaneous; the page metadata says Oct. 14, 2006, but the content covers City of Ember's Oct. 10–12, 2008 opening and the copyright line says 2008, so the real date is mid-October 2008]
- The Seeker: The Dark Is Rising (Oct. 5, 2007) was the first Fox-Walden release. It opened in 3,141 theaters to about $3.7–3.8M (7th place), grossed about $8.8M domestic and about $31.4–31.9M worldwide on a budget estimated at $40–45M. Reports say the result led Fox to reconsider adapting the rest of the series — [Wikipedia: The Seeker](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/The_Seeker_(film)); [The Numbers](https://www.the-numbers.com/movie/Seeker-The-Dark-is-Rising-The-(2007)) [retrospective]
- Disney declined to co-produce or co-finance the third Narnia film (Voyage of the Dawn Treader), citing "budgetary and logistical reasons" (per The Hollywood Reporter). The same article says Walden's box-office record was mixed, that City of Ember "bombing" had observers wondering "if Walden might be in trouble," and that some speculated Fox might end the partnership. It adds that Walden, which owns the Narnia film rights, would likely need a partner such as Fox for a film budgeted near $200M — [Christianity Today, "Disney Dumps Narnia," Dec. 25, 2008](https://www.christianitytoday.com/2008/12/disney-dumps-narnia/) [contemporaneous]
- Cary Granat stepped down as co-CEO effective Dec. 1, 2008, and moved to a consulting role (including the Narnia franchise and AEG Live). Michael Bostick (hired March 2008 from Imagine) and Anschutz Film Group CEO David Weil took over his duties (per a Walden press release, Nov. 14, 2008; LA Times Company Town blog, Claudia Eller, "Granat out at Walden Media," Nov. 14, 2008; Hollywood Reporter) — reported via search snippets of [Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/business/business-news/walden-original-has-folded-his-123072/) and [Wikipedia: Walden Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walden_Media) [contemporaneous originals; full text not accessed]. Variety described the split as mutual, with Granat staying on as a consultant, and called City of Ember one of Walden's costly flops — snippet of [Variety, "Cary Granat to leave Walden Media," 2008](https://variety.com/2008/film/markets-festivals/cary-granat-to-leave-walden-media-1117995874/) [contemporaneous; paywalled, snippet only]

### Inferences
- In 2008 Walden's hits came from outside Fox: Prince Caspian with Disney and Journey with Warner/New Line. Inside the Fox-Walden arrangement, its run of results was weak. That weakens any simple claim that "Walden was doing well in 2008."
- Granat's exit in November 2008 came about five weeks after City of Ember opened and six weeks after the Fox-Walden restructuring. No source I accessed makes the film the explicit cause.

### Gaps
- I found no reliable box-office figures for Nim's Island, Mr. Magorium or Journey in this research pass (another researcher may have them).
- I did not access the full text of the Variety and LA Times pieces on Granat's exit.

## 2. Fox-Walden: formation, terms, films, wind-down

### Takeaway
Fox Filmed Entertainment and Walden Media announced the joint venture on Aug. 8–9, 2006. It was at heart a family-film marketing label, with Fox distributing everything it released. Four films came out under the Fox-Walden banner: The Seeker (Oct. 2007), Mr. Magorium's Wonder Emporium (Nov. 2007), Nim's Island (Apr. 2008) and City of Ember (Oct. 10, 2008). On about Oct. 2–5, 2008, a week before City of Ember opened, the venture was "restructured and streamlined" into an in-house Fox unit, with roughly a dozen layoffs. City of Ember was released under the Fox-Walden banner.

### Cited Findings
- **Formation (Aug. 2006):** 20th Century Fox and Walden Media formed a joint venture to make and market G- and PG-rated family films, with titles contributed by both companies. It gave Walden "a permanent home for future projects" while Walden stayed independent with its own production staff. Walden's existing projects with Disney, Paramount and New Line would continue. The venture had no name yet — [NarniaWeb, Aug. 8, 2006, reprinting MercuryNews.com](https://www.narniaweb.com/?p=4079) [contemporaneous]
- Per Daily Variety, as quoted by TMZ: "The new label will handle only marketing," and "Fox will distribute all the titles the new company markets." Development and production stayed with Walden. The first titles named were Mr. Magorium's Wonder Emporium and The Dark Is Rising — [TMZ, Aug. 9, 2006](https://www.tmz.com/2006/08/09/ninteenth-century-fox) [contemporaneous, quoting Variety]
- Fox would contribute current and newly developed projects, and Walden its future development slate. Fox also received first-look rights to projects from Walden's sister company Bristol Bay Productions — search snippet summarizing [Wikipedia: Walden Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walden_Media) and [Screen Daily, "Walden, Fox give birth to family film label," 2006](https://www.screendaily.com/walden-fox-give-birth-to-family-film-label/4028232.article) [Screen Daily is contemporaneous but was blocked; snippet only]
- An earlier step: Walden signed a five-picture production deal with 20th Century Fox on July 12, 2004 (citing a Walden press release and Variety, July 12, 2004). The Aug. 9, 2006 deal covered education-themed G/PG films released under the 20th Century Fox banner. "Fox-Walden" announced its first four films in May 2007 (citing Hollywood Reporter, Borys Kit, May 17, 2007) — [Wikipedia: Walden Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walden_Media) [retrospective]
- Wikipedia's entry for The Seeker describes the venture as one in which the two studios "co-financed and marketed films as 'Fox Walden'" — [Wikipedia: The Seeker](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/The_Seeker_(film)) [retrospective; no specific financing split given]
- **Restructuring (early Oct. 2008):** Walden Media and Fox Filmed Entertainment announced the "restructuring and streamlining" of Fox Walden. The venture would be housed within Fox rather than run as a standalone entity. Each company would keep developing family films separately and could offer the other co-finance and co-production opportunities. Fox Walden would still oversee those films and Fox would still distribute them domestically. The original target of 3–5 films a year might shrink slightly. Fox Walden chief Jeffrey Godsick rejoined Fox as EVP of marketing and digital content while keeping his Fox Walden title. Continuing projects were Tooth Fairy and Ramona. Earlier releases named were The Seeker, Mr. Magorium and Nim's Island, with City of Ember upcoming — [Dark Horizons, Oct. 5, 2008](https://www.darkhorizons.com/?p=59669) [contemporaneous]
- Wikipedia dates the announcement to Oct. 2, 2008 (citing MovieWeb, Brian Gallagher, Oct. 2, 2008). It says the company reduced staff and stopped operating standalone because its films had underperformed in North America, and was folded into 20th Century Fox as a division while the partnership continued — [Wikipedia: Walden Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walden_Media) [retrospective]
- Variety reported that about a dozen Fox Walden staffers would be laid off, a detail Fox's own announcement did not disclose. The move consolidated Fox film marketing resources — search snippets of [Screen Daily, "Fox Walden restructure sees Godsick move back into Fox"](https://www.screendaily.com/fox-walden-restructure-sees-godsick-move-back-into-fox/4041228.article) and [Hollywood Reporter, "Godsick moves..."](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/business/business-news/godsick-moves-fox-walden-145889/) [contemporaneous; paywalled]. One snippet (via [AWN](https://www.awn.com/news/fox-walden-absorbs-fox)) notes that only three films had been released under the logo (Nim's Island, Mr. Magorium, The Seeker), which was not enough to sustain a standalone marketing entity [contemporaneous; snippet only]
- Christianity Today, citing Variety: Fox absorbed Walden's marketing division as an in-house unit, ending it as a standalone company. About a dozen staffers were laid off and Godsick returned to Fox. Anschutz Film Group CEO David Weil said the Walden brand "isn't going anywhere," called Fox "an outstanding partner," and said Walden intended to keep making 3–5 films a year. Variety reported no Fox Walden films planned for 2009, but a Fox release and the Hollywood Reporter said Tooth Fairy (Dwayne Johnson) was shooting. Ramona was targeted for 2010 — [Christianity Today, mid-Oct 2008](https://www.christianitytoday.com/2006/10/walden) [contemporaneous]
- **City of Ember's banner:** Released Oct. 10, 2008 by 20th Century Fox "under the 'Fox-Walden' joint-venture" — [Wikipedia: City of Ember](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/City_of_Ember) [retrospective]. A Screen Daily snippet (Cannes 2007 sales story) says "Fox-Walden will also handle City Of Ember... Fox will distribute in North America through its marketing and distribution arrangement with Walden" — [Screen Daily](https://www.screendaily.com/summit-back-at-cannes-with-walden-polanski-step-up-2/4032107.article) [contemporaneous; snippet only]. Christianity Today (Oct. 2008) calls it "the Fox Walden film" [contemporaneous]
- **Later partnership:** Fox distributed Tooth Fairy (Jan. 2010), Ramona and Beezus (July 2010) and The Voyage of the Dawn Treader (Dec. 2010) — [Wikipedia: Walden Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walden_Media) [retrospective]

### Inferences
- The restructuring was announced roughly one week before City of Ember opened, so it cannot have been a reaction to the film's opening weekend. It reflected the weak results of the earlier three Fox-Walden titles. City of Ember's flop then fed later commentary, such as Christianity Today's Dec. 2008 speculation that Fox might drop Walden.
- The Fox–Walden relationship survived in a slimmer form: a marketing unit absorbed into Fox, with co-financing offered case by case. Fox later took over Narnia from Disney.

### Gaps
- **Financial terms are not public in anything I accessed.** I found no reported cost split, revenue split, distribution fee or P&A-funding arrangement for the 2006 deal or for City of Ember specifically. Sources describe it variously as "marketing-only" (Variety via TMZ, 2006) and as co-financing (Wikipedia, retrospective). Do not state a split.
- The exact Oct. 2008 announcement date: Oct. 2 per Wikipedia/MovieWeb, while Dark Horizons posted Oct. 5. The full original press release was not found.

## 3. Production: Playtone, development, Belfast shoot, budget and incentives

### Takeaway
City of Ember was a Walden Media / Playtone (Tom Hanks and Gary Goetzman) co-production, directed by Gil Kenan (his live-action debut after the animated Monster House, 2006) from a Caroline Thompson script. It was shot over about 14–16 weeks in summer and fall 2007 at the Paint Hall, a former Harland & Wolff shipyard building in Belfast's Titanic Quarter. Northern Ireland Screen put in £800,000, and the production spent about £9–9.2M locally. The reported budget is $55M in most databases, against $38M in Christianity Today (Oct. 2008).

### Cited Findings
- Walden is "co-producing with Tom Hanks and Gary Goetzman's Playtone Productions" — snippet of [Screen Daily, "Summit sales soar with deals on hot titles," 2007](https://www.screendaily.com/summit-sales-soar-with-deals-on-hot-titles/4032634.article) [contemporaneous; snippet only]. A Screen Daily headline, "Playtone and Walden to shoot City of Ember in Northern Ireland," exists ([link](https://www.screendaily.com/playtone-and-walden-to-shoot-city-of-ember-in-northern-ireland/4031420.article), 2007) but its text was blocked
- Kenan's previous film was the motion-capture animated Monster House (2006), and City of Ember was his live-action debut. Caroline Thompson wrote the screenplay from Jeanne DuPrau's 2003 novel — [Moria Reviews](https://www.moriareviews.com/?p=1658); [Wikipedia: City of Ember](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/City_of_Ember) [retrospective]
- 14-week shoot at the Paint Hall in Belfast's Harland and Wolff estate (set included Ember Square), spending more than £9M in the Belfast area, "the biggest single investment in the Northern economy by a film" — [Irish Times, "The North basks in movie lights," Apr. 19, 2008](https://www.irishtimes.com/news/the-north-basks-in-movie-lights-1.915048) [contemporaneous]
- An estimated £9.2M went into the local economy, and Northern Ireland Screen provided £800,000. NI Screen leased the Paint Hall for three years at £200,000 a year (through 2010) and bought the rigging and lighting City of Ember had used. By June 2008 the stage had been empty for seven months despite an offer of free use plus up to £500,000 in funding — [4NI, "Paint Hall studio burned out after City of Ember," June 24, 2008](https://www.4ni.co.uk/northern-ireland-news/78039/paint-hall-studio-burned-out-after-city-of-ember) [contemporaneous]
- Filming was scheduled from early summer 2007 to October 2007, described as a 16-week shoot (Wikipedia) versus 14 weeks (Irish Times). One studio history says the stage was used July–October 2007 — [Wikipedia: City of Ember](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Ember); [The Studio Tour: The Paint Hall](https://www.thestudiotour.com/wp/studios/the-paint-hall) [retrospective]
- Budget: $55M per [Box Office Mojo](https://www.boxofficemojo.com/title/tt0970411) [retrospective]. $38M production budget per [Christianity Today, mid-Oct 2008](https://www.christianitytoday.com/2006/10/walden) [contemporaneous] and [The Numbers](https://www.the-numbers.com/movie/City-of-Ember) [retrospective]. **The sources conflict, and none I accessed explains the gap** (it may be gross versus net of incentives, or include overhead)

### Inferences
- The Northern Ireland incentives (£800K from NI Screen plus the UK film tax relief of the time) were small next to the budget. The bigger local story was the Paint Hall becoming a lasting studio asset; it later hosted Your Highness (2009) and, retrospectively, Game of Thrones.

### Gaps
- I found no contemporaneous source on **how or when the book rights were acquired**, or on the timing of Playtone's involvement. Wikipedia (retrospective) says Playtone also bought the rights to The People of Sparks. No reliable date found for Kenan's or Thompson's hiring.
- No contemporaneous source confirms whether UK film tax credits were claimed, or for how much.
- I found no source splitting the financing between Walden and Playtone. One low-quality, inaccessible site claimed it was "financed by Walden Media."

## 4. International distribution

### Takeaway
Fox distributed in North America under Fox-Walden. Summit Entertainment's international arm handled foreign sales, and the film was pre-sold territory by territory to independent distributors. Entertainment Film Distributors released it in the UK on Oct. 10, 2008, the same day as the US.

### Cited Findings
- At Cannes 2007, Summit's international sales arm unveiled a slate including two Walden titles, Nim's Island and City of Ember. Fox would distribute in North America via its arrangement with Walden — snippet of [Screen Daily, "Summit back at Cannes with Walden, Polanski, Step Up 2"](https://www.screendaily.com/summit-back-at-cannes-with-walden-polanski-step-up-2/4032107.article) [contemporaneous, May 2007; snippet only]
- "Entertainment is believed to have swooped on UK rights to City Of Ember" — snippet of [Screen Daily, "Summit sales soar with deals on hot titles"](https://www.screendaily.com/summit-sales-soar-with-deals-on-hot-titles/4032634.article) [contemporaneous, 2007; snippet only]
- IMDb lists Entertainment Film Distributors as UK theatrical distributor (2008), and Summit International as sales agent. UK release date Oct. 10, 2008 per Time Out / Box Office Mojo — [IMDb company credits](https://www.imdb.de/title/tt0970411/companycredits); [Time Out](https://timeout.com/london/film/city-of-ember) [retrospective databases]. Wikipedia lists "Summit Entertainment (International)" as distributor — [Wikipedia: Walden Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walden_Media) [retrospective]. TCM also lists 20th Century Fox International among the distributors — [TCM](https://tcm.com/tcmdb/title/665708/wd) [retrospective; the territory is not specified]

### Inferences
- The structure (domestic studio distribution plus foreign pre-sales through Summit) matches Walden's earlier Bridge to Terabithia and Nim's Island, which also list Summit. Pre-sales would have shifted some international risk off Walden and Playtone.

### Gaps
- I found no full territory-by-territory list of buyers beyond the UK (Entertainment). I could not confirm which territories, if any, Fox International handled.

## 5. 2008 market context

### Takeaway
City of Ember opened Oct. 10, 2008, in the week of the global stock-market crash, into a weekend led by Disney's Beverly Hills Chihuahua (second weekend). It made about $3.1M from 2,022 theaters (11th place). I found no contemporaneous trade piece that explicitly blamed the financial crisis for its failure.

### Cited Findings
- Weekend of Oct. 10–12, 2008: Beverly Hills Chihuahua was No. 1 again with about $17.5M (−40%), and Quarantine opened No. 2 with $14.2M. City of Ember opened to $3,129,473 from 2,022 theaters, finishing 11th — [Box Office Mojo weekend 41](https://www.boxofficemojo.com/weekend/2008W41); [The Numbers weekend chart](https://www.the-numbers.com/box-office-chart/weekend/2008/10/10) [retrospective databases of contemporaneous data]
- City of Ember averaged about $1,500 per screen and finished just behind Fireproof (made for $500K). David Poland (Movie City News) called it "an unmitigated distribution car wreck" and predicted the end of Fox Walden as a production company — [Christianity Today, mid-Oct 2008](https://www.christianitytoday.com/2006/10/walden) [contemporaneous]
- Domestic total $7,873,007, worldwide about $17.9M — [Box Office Mojo](https://www.boxofficemojo.com/title/tt0970411) [retrospective]

### Inferences
- The Fox-Walden downsizing (Oct. 2–5) and the release fell in the same weeks as the September–October 2008 market crash. Linking the two is reasonable only as timing; no source I accessed claims causation. The thin marketing push implied by Poland's comment fits Fox having just folded the venture's marketing staff in-house.

### Gaps
- I found no contemporaneous 2008 trade coverage tying studio reactions to the financial crisis to family films or to City of Ember specifically. That needs a targeted search, e.g. LA Times/NYT late-2008 coverage of studio cutbacks.
- No source covers the fall 2008 family slate beyond Beverly Hills Chihuahua (e.g. Madagascar 2 in Nov., Bolt in Nov.) in a contemporaneous framing.

## 6. Coverage linking City of Ember to Walden/Fox strategy changes and the People of Sparks sequel

### Takeaway
Contemporaneous coverage (Variety, Christianity Today) put City of Ember among Walden's "costly flops" and raised doubts about Walden's future. The structural Fox-Walden change came before release, and Granat's exit came about five weeks after. The shelving of a People of Sparks sequel appears only in retrospective sources.

### Cited Findings
- Variety (Granat exit story, Nov. 2008) called City of Ember one of Walden's costly flops and noted the Fox Walden downsizing — snippet of [Variety](https://variety.com/2008/film/markets-festivals/cary-granat-to-leave-walden-media-1117995874/) [contemporaneous; paywalled, snippet only]
- After the opening, Walden's "viability as a film studio is in question," and Poland predicted the end of Fox Walden as a production company. Anschutz Film Group CEO David Weil said the brand isn't going anywhere — [Christianity Today, mid-Oct 2008](https://www.christianitytoday.com/2006/10/walden) [contemporaneous]
- The City of Ember "bombing" prompted "industry observers to wonder if Walden might be in trouble" — [Christianity Today, Dec. 25, 2008](https://www.christianitytoday.com/2008/12/disney-dumps-narnia/) [contemporaneous]
- Playtone had purchased or optioned the rights to The People of Sparks, but the sequel was shelved after the first film's box-office failure — [Wikipedia: The People of Sparks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_People_of_Sparks) [retrospective]. Before release, cast members including Saoirse Ronan and Tim Robbins told io9 they were open to returning — [Gizmodo/io9, "Will there be a City of Ember 2?"](https://gizmodo.com/will-there-be-a-city-of-ember-2-5061434) [contemporaneous, 2008]. None of DuPrau's other Ember novels has since been adapted — [SF Encyclopedia](https://sf-encyclopedia.com/entry/city_of_ember) [retrospective]
- Later context: in Aug. 2013 Walden laid off more than a dozen of about 50 staff, ended original development and moved to co-financing third-party projects — [Deadline via Yahoo, Aug. 1, 2013](https://ca.finance.yahoo.com/news/layoffs-hit-chronicles-narnia-producer-walden-media-174723268.html) [retrospective]

### Inferences
- The likely story: Fox-Walden's standalone structure had already collapsed under the 2007–early-2008 results. City of Ember's failure confirmed the gloomy reading, coincided with Granat's exit and the loss of Disney on Narnia, and ended any sequel plans. Walden survived as a Fox co-financing partner, not as a semi-autonomous label.

### Gaps
- No contemporaneous (2008–2009) trade article was found that explicitly announces the cancellation of a People of Sparks film. The "shelved" claim rests on Wikipedia, and its sourcing was not checked.
- I found no source that explicitly attributes Granat's departure to City of Ember.
