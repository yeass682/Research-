## 1. What did major critics say in their original 2008 reviews?

### Takeaway
Critics were split, mostly toward mild approval. They consistently praised the production design and visuals and the cast, especially Ronan, though some said she was wasted. The main complaints were that the film was thin on action and adventure, generic or "serviceable" as an adaptation, and derivative. Opinions on Bill Murray's mayor ranged from "wonderful" to "completely wrong for the role."

### Cited Findings
- **New York Times, Stephen Holden, Oct 10, 2008** (headline "Fleeing a Dying Civilization, Toward Hope and Sunlight"). Wrote that the "talents of Saoirse Ronan, the brilliant young actress from Atonement" were "wasted in the science-fiction juvenilia of City of Ember." This is quoted through Wikipedia, not read on nytimes.com. A FilmAffinity excerpt gives the NYT line "Saoirse Ronan has nothing to work with." — [Wikipedia: City of Ember (film)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Ember_(film)); [FilmAffinity review excerpts](https://m.filmaffinity.com/ie/moviereviews.php?id=876990)
- **Variety, Justin Chang, Oct 13, 2008** (headline "Eye-popping 'Ember' burns out"). Called it "a good-looking but no more than serviceable adaptation" of the novel. He also remarked that the characters represent "English-speaking, predominantly Caucasian humanity." Quoted through Wikipedia. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Ember_(film))
- **USA Today, Claudia Puig** (2008; exact date not verified): "City of Ember provides audiences with a thought-provoking and family-friendly adventure." — [Rotten Tomatoes](https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/city_of_ember)

### Inferences
- The design and craft praise is consistent across both positive and negative reviews, and it is the only area that drew award nominations.
- Of the top critics who could be checked, the NYT and Variety were the most dismissive. Regional and family-oriented critics (TV Guide, SF Chronicle, Chicago Tribune, Washington Post, USA Today) were moderately positive. Many reviews framed it as a decent "family film" with some appeal for older kids and adults.

## 2. Aggregate scores (contemporaneous vs current)

### Takeaway
The aggregates are "mixed": Rotten Tomatoes is around 52–53% and Metacritic is 58. Opening-night audiences were reasonably satisfied (CinemaScore B+), while the current RT audience score is lower (46%).

### Cited Findings
- **Rotten Tomatoes (current, retrieved Nov 2026):** 52% from 124 reviews. Consensus: "City of Ember is visually arresting, and boasts a superb cast, but is sadly lacking in both action and adventure." Popcornmeter (audience): 46% from 50,000+ ratings. — [Rotten Tomatoes](https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/city_of_ember)
  - **Conflict:** Wikipedia cites 53% from 129 reviews, average 5.8/10 (retrieved Apr 8, 2025), and other mirrors cite 54% from 130. The score has moved slightly over time. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Ember_(film))
- **Metacritic:** 58/100 from 27 critics, "Mixed or average" (17 positive, 9 mixed, 1 negative). Wikipedia's citation for the 58 was retrieved Aug 12, 2009, so the score is effectively contemporaneous. — [Metacritic](https://www.metacritic.com/movie/city-of-ember/critic-reviews/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Ember_(film))
- **CinemaScore:** B+, collected from opening-weekend audiences in Oct 2008. Sourced through Wikipedia, which cites an archived CinemaScore search page. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Ember_(film))

### Inferences
- A B+ CinemaScore is middling-to-decent and doesn't suggest audience hostility. The film's problem was getting people into theaters, not their reaction once there.
- The critics' scores (Metacritic 17 of 27 positive) are slightly warmer than the RT percentage suggests.

### Gaps
- **IMDb rating:** not retrieved, and there is no 2008 snapshot.
- **CinemaScore in 2008 trade reports:** no 2008 weekend report mentioning the grade was found.

## 3. Audience and book-fan reaction; Jeanne DuPrau's view

### Takeaway
DuPrau was politely mixed. She found the film "visually marvelous" but felt "some of the story's power was sacrificed to speed and spectacle." Book-world commentators, such as a literary agent's blog, felt the adaptation had "mucked" up a "brilliant book."

### Cited Findings
- **Jeanne DuPrau, interview with Slice of SciFi (Michael Hickerson), Nov 13, 2008:** "It's visually marvelous, fast-paced and well-acted, though it seemed to me that some of the story's power was sacrificed to speed and spectacle." She also said the director "has added things I wouldn't have added and subtracted things I would have left in," and that she had little input beyond commenting on an early draft. — [Slice of SciFi](https://www.sliceofscifi.com/2008/11/13/duprau-talks-about-city-of-ember/)
- **Kristin Nelson (literary agent, Pub Rants blog), Oct 14, 2008:** defended the source: "CITY OF EMBER is a brilliant book" and "it's not Jeanne DuPrau's fault they mucked it so get thee a copy." A reader comment (Oct 15, 2008) complained that the trailer gave away the twist that the city "has survived for two hundred years under ground." — [Nelson Literary Agency](https://nelsonagency.com/2008/10/when-a-movie-heads-south)
- **The added mole creature** was singled out even by sympathetic critics. io9 (Oct 10, 2008) called the giant tentacle-faced mole a surprising addition. At the junket, Bill Murray joked that being eaten by the mole might not mean his character was dead. — [io9 review](https://gizmodo.com/ember-proves-the-only-grown-up-movies-are-aimed-at-kids-5061881); [io9 sequel piece](https://gizmodo.com/will-there-be-a-city-of-ember-2-5061434)
- **Pre-release publishing context:** Publishers Weekly (John A. Sellers, Sep 11, 2008) noted the Oct 10 release. Random House Children's editorial director Jim Thomas said, "We all hope it will do well." — [Publishers Weekly](https://www2.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industry-news/page-to-screen/article/9793-movie-alert-city-of-ember.html)
- **Librarian and fan blogs** (e.g., 100 Scope Notes, Oct 12, 2008; CA Library edublog, Dec 7, 2008) reviewed the film. One family-blog reviewer said the film "lost its plot" near the ending. These were seen only as search snippets. — [100 Scope Notes](https://100scopenotes.com/2008/10/12/movie-review-city-of-ember); [CA Library edublog](https://calibrary.edublogs.org/2008/12/07/review-of-the-city-of-ember/)

### Inferences
- Book fans and book-world insiders were likely the film's most critical audience on fidelity. The added spectacle (the mole, the chase sequences) was the main point of friction, and DuPrau's "speed and spectacle" remark matches that.

### Gaps
- **Unverified claim:** a search-engine summary claimed that a Publishers Weekly piece quoted DuPrau's editor saying she and DuPrau were "concerned that the subtleties of the book had been lost in translation" after reading the screenplay. The PW article actually fetched (Sep 11, 2008) does not contain this. Treat as unverified.

## 4. Why it underperformed: contemporaneous commentary

### Takeaway
2008 commentary blamed thin marketing from the Fox Walden joint venture ("working without an ad budget"), a crowded October weekend, and Fox's broader slump. The film opened 11th with $3.1M, behind the $500K Christian film *Fireproof*. Within days Fox folded Fox Walden's marketing into its in-house division.

### Cited Findings
- **Opening weekend (Oct 10–12, 2008):** #11, $3,129,473 from 2,022 theaters, about $1,547 per theater. Competition that weekend: *Beverly Hills Chihuahua* ($17.5M, second week at #1), *Quarantine* ($14.2M), *Body of Lies* ($12.9M), *Eagle Eye*, *Nick and Norah's Infinite Playlist*, *The Express*, and *Fireproof* (#10, $3.14M). — [Box Office Mojo, 2008 Weekend 41](https://www.boxofficemojo.com/weekend/2008W41)
- **Christianity Today, Peter T. Chattaway, "Is Walden in Trouble?" (Oct 2008; the page shows a wrong 2006 date, but its content and copyright are 2008):**
  - The per-screen average of about $1,500 was near the bottom of 125 wide releases that year.
  - Box-office analyst David Poland said the Fox Walden crew seemed to be "working without an ad budget," called it "an unmitigated distribution car wreck," and predicted the end of Fox Walden.
  - It cites LA Times columnist Patrick Goldstein on "the Fox bad movie streak."
  - Fox Walden's marketing arm was being folded into Fox's in-house marketing, with about a dozen layoffs.
  - Walden CEO David Weil said "Fox Walden has been a tremendous asset for our company."

  — [Christianity Today](https://www.christianitytoday.com/?p=4760)
- **Second weekend:** fell about 44.7% to $1,730,755. Domestic total was about $7.87M. — [Box Office Flops](https://www.boxofficeflops.com/yearly-breakdowns/2008-2/city-of-ember/); [The Numbers](https://www.the-numbers.com/movie/City-of-Ember)
- **Worldwide total:** about $17.9M against a reported $55M budget, labelled a box-office bomb. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Ember_(film))
  - **Budget conflict:** The Numbers and Christianity Today give $38M. — [The Numbers](https://www.the-numbers.com/movie/City-of-Ember); [Christianity Today](https://www.christianitytoday.com/?p=4760)
- **Retrospective/secondary:** Box Office Flops attributes the weak opening partly to "scaled back marketing from FOX." Moria Reviews calls it Walden Media's least financially successful film. — [Box Office Flops](https://www.boxofficeflops.com/yearly-breakdowns/2008-2/city-of-ember/); [Moria](https://www.moriareviews.com/?p=1658)

### Inferences
- The low CinemaScore-vs-turnout gap (B+ but $1.5K per screen) points to awareness and marketing failure, not word of mouth. *Beverly Hills Chihuahua* held the family audience that weekend.
- Several reviews pitched the film to "older kids and adults." This hints at the target-audience confusion that the "family film" positioning may have caused. This is an inference, not a sourced 2008 claim.

### Gaps
- **No 2008 trade article was found** that explicitly discusses release-date choice or target-audience confusion. The Variety piece on the Fox Walden restructuring was referenced but not fetched.
- **UK release date** was not confirmed. A Camden New Journal review dated Oct 9, 2008 suggests a UK release around Oct 10, 2008, close to the US date rather than "later." This needs verification.

## 5. Sequel (The People of Sparks)

### Takeaway
At the October 2008 junket, the cast and director explicitly made a sequel depend on box office. After the flop, no sequel was made. I found no formal 2008 cancellation announcement; reports of it being "shelved" are secondary and later.

### Cited Findings
- **Original rights deal:** In Oct 2004, Playtone (Tom Hanks, Gary Goetzman) bought the film rights in the mid-six figures, with an option on *The People of Sparks*. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Ember_(film))
- **io9, Meredith Woerner, Oct 10, 2008:**
  - Ronan said she would return "if Gil was still going to do it and we still had all of the cast." She relayed Kenan's line to the cast: "Get all of your friends to go and see it and then we'll see."
  - Tim Robbins: "Apparently, both characters are in the next book."
  - Murray joked, "I see myself in a sling in the sequel."

  — [io9/Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/will-there-be-a-city-of-ember-2-5061434)
- **Wikipedia's article on The People of Sparks** says that after the first film's box-office failure, plans for the sequel were shelved. This is retrospective and secondary. — [Wikipedia: The People of Sparks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_People_of_Sparks)

### Inferences
- The sequel died quietly. No official cancellation was found in 2008–09 coverage, consistent with an option simply not being exercised.

### Gaps
- No dated 2008–09 report of a formal cancellation was found.
