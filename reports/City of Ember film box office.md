# City of Ember Flopped Despite Decent Reviews

*City of Ember* (2008) grossed **$7.87 million in the US and Canada, about $10 million abroad, and about $17.9 million worldwide**. It sold roughly another **$8.4 million of DVDs in the US**. The reported production budget was **$38 million** in the one 2008 source that gives a figure and **$55 million** in the databases that later became standard ([Box Office Mojo](https://www.boxofficemojo.com/title/tt0970411/); [The Numbers](https://www.the-numbers.com/movie/City-of-Ember); [Christianity Today, Oct 2008](https://www.christianitytoday.com/2006/10/walden)). The film was a clear commercial flop, and 2008 observers said so. TicketNews reported "depressed ticket sales" on opening weekend, and box-office analyst David Poland called the release "an unmitigated distribution car wreck." It was not disliked, though. Critics were mixed to mildly positive (Metacritic 58, with 17 of 27 reviews positive), and opening-weekend audiences gave it a **B+ CinemaScore**. The problem was that few people knew about it or turned up. The timing made this worse: Fox folded the Fox-Walden marketing label into its own operation about a week before release and laid off roughly a dozen staff. The film then opened 11th on 2,022 screens and lost three-quarters of its theaters by its third weekend. When I apply standard 2008 deal mechanics (theaters keep roughly half the ticket money, foreign rentals are lower, and marketing and prints are paid on top of the budget), I estimate the film lost **roughly $25–65 million across everyone who paid for it, with a central estimate of $35–55 million**, even after DVD and TV income. These are estimates. No studio ever published the real accounts, and several key 2008 trade sources were paywalled or blocked during this research.

Box Office Mojo and The Numbers agree on domestic. The domestic run was short and front-loaded. Box Office Guru had projected about $6 million beforehand, so the film earned roughly half of a modest forecast ([RT/Box Office Guru, Oct 9, 2008](https://editorial.rottentomatoes.com/article/box-office-guru-preview-audiences-surrounded-by-lies/)). Opening weekend ended up as **39.7% of the film's entire domestic gross**, and **89% of domestic money came in during October 2008** ([BOM October 2008](https://www.boxofficemojo.com/month/october/2008/)).

## 2008 coverage called it a "car wreck," but critics and audiences were lukewarm, not hostile

Here is how each kind of source describes the result:

- **Contemporaneous, before release.** Box Office Guru's Gitesh Pandya said on Oct 9, 2008 that it "will be a tough sell." He also said "the marketing push has not been too forceful," and that fantasy films had struggled in the US "in the post-Hobbit world" ([RT/Box Office Guru](https://editorial.rottentomatoes.com/article/box-office-guru-preview-audiences-surrounded-by-lies/)).
- **Contemporaneous, opening weekend.** TicketNews reported that Ember and *The Express* "reported depressed ticket sales" ([TicketNews](https://www.ticketnews.com/2008/10/beverly-hills-chihuahua-remains-the-alpha-dog-at-the-box-office/)).
- **Contemporaneous, the sharpest verdict.** This came from Christianity Today's Peter T. Chattaway in mid-October 2008, in a piece titled "Is Walden in Trouble?" He noted the film's **~$1,500 per-screen average sat near the bottom of the year's 125 wide releases**. He quoted David Poland saying the Fox Walden team seemed to be "working without an ad budget" and calling the release "an unmitigated distribution car wreck." The online page is misdated 2006, but its content and copyright are 2008 ([Christianity Today](https://www.christianitytoday.com/2006/10/walden)).
- **Contemporaneous, by December.** The same outlet said Ember's "bombing" had "industry observers [wondering] if Walden might be in trouble" ([Christianity Today, Dec 25, 2008](https://www.christianitytoday.com/2008/12/disney-dumps-narnia/)).
- **Contemporaneous, home-video reviews.** January 2009 DVD reviewers called the ~$7.9 million gross "dismal" (wording via search summary) ([DVD Talk](https://www.dvdtalk.com/reviews/35993/city-of-ember/); [Inside Pulse, Jan 31, 2009](https://insidepulse.com/2009/01/31/city-of-ember-dvd-review/)).
- **Retrospective.** The explicit phrase **"box-office bomb"** comes mainly from later sources: Wikipedia, and the Bomb Report site, which wrote in 2015 that "FOX dumped the film" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Ember); [Bomb Report](https://bombreport.com/yearly-breakdowns/2008-2/city-of-ember/)).

There are real holes in the 2008 record. I could not retrieve any Variety, Hollywood Reporter, LA Times, New York Times or AP weekend box-office story from Oct 12–13, 2008. Deadline Hollywood Daily's weekend report sat behind a paywall redirect. Search summaries suggest it called Ember a weak, costly would-be franchise launch, but I could not read the text, so I don't quote it ([Deadline](https://deadline.com/2008/10/dicaprio-crowe-race-dog-pic-for-friday-no-1-7231/)). A Variety story on Walden co-CEO Cary Granat's November 2008 exit apparently listed Ember among Walden's "costly flops," but I saw only a snippet ([Variety](https://variety.com/2008/film/markets-festivals/cary-granat-to-leave-walden-media-1117995874/)). The Wayback Machine, which might have recovered Box Office Mojo's own 2008 weekend write-up, was blocked.

On whether people liked it, the answer is a qualified yes:

- **Craft praise.** Critics consistently praised the production design and the young cast.
- **Main complaints.** They objected that the story was thin on action and the adaptation generic.
- **Negative reviews.** Variety's Justin Chang called it "a good-looking but no more than serviceable adaptation" under the headline "Eye-popping 'Ember' burns out." The New York Times' Stephen Holden said Saoirse Ronan's talents were "wasted" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Ember)).
- **Positive reviews.** The San Francisco Chronicle called it "an impressive and imaginative fantasy," and TV Guide called it "a fun and moving family film with a subtly dark feel" ([Metacritic](https://www.metacritic.com/movie/city-of-ember/critic-reviews/)).
- **Scores.** Metacritic's **58** was already on record by August 2009. The **B+ CinemaScore** was collected from opening-night audiences. Rotten Tomatoes today shows **52% from critics and 46% from audiences**, a retrospective measure ([Rotten Tomatoes](https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/city_of_ember)).
- **The author.** Jeanne DuPrau found it "visually marvelous, fast-paced and well-acted," but felt "some of the story's power was sacrificed to speed and spectacle" ([Slice of SciFi, Nov 13, 2008](https://www.sliceofscifi.com/2008/11/13/duprau-talks-about-city-of-ember/)).

A B+ grade alongside a bottom-tier per-screen average points to an awareness problem, not a word-of-mouth problem. The people who came were reasonably satisfied, but very few came.

## Who made it: a Walden–Playtone film sold through a label Fox dissolved a week before opening

A movie's credits hide several different businesses. Ember's chain looked like this:

- **Producers and creative team.** **Walden Media** co-produced with **Playtone**, Tom Hanks and Gary Goetzman's company ([Screen Daily snippet, 2007](https://www.screendaily.com/summit-sales-soar-with-deals-on-hot-titles/4032634.article)). Playtone had bought the novel's rights in October 2004 for a mid-six-figure sum, with an option on the sequel *The People of Sparks* ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Ember)). Gil Kenan directed; it was his first live-action film after *Monster House*. Caroline Thompson wrote the script ([Moria Reviews](https://www.moriareviews.com/?p=1658)).
- **Walden's owner.** Walden was majority-owned by Philip Anschutz's group. It specialized in "life affirming" literary adaptations ([Wikipedia: Walden Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walden_Media)).
- **The shoot.** The film was made over about 14 weeks in 2007 in the Paint Hall, a former Harland & Wolff shipyard building in Belfast. It spent **more than £9 million locally**, "the biggest single investment in the Northern economy by a film" ([Irish Times, Apr 19, 2008](https://www.irishtimes.com/news/the-north-basks-in-movie-lights-1.915048)). Northern Ireland Screen contributed **£800,000** ([4NI, June 24, 2008](https://www.4ni.co.uk/northern-ireland-news/78039/paint-hall-studio-burned-out-after-city-of-ember)).
- **Financing split.** No source I found divides the financing between Walden and Playtone.

The **distribution** layer is where the corporate turmoil sat. In August 2006, Walden and 20th Century Fox created a family-film joint venture. Daily Variety described the new label as one that "will handle only marketing," with "Fox [to] distribute all the titles" ([TMZ quoting Variety, Aug 9, 2006](https://www.tmz.com/2006/08/09/ninteenth-century-fox)). Later summaries describe the two companies as co-financing the label's films ([Wikipedia: The Seeker](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/The_Seeker_(film))). No cost or revenue split was ever made public.

The label's first three releases all disappointed: *The Seeker* (Oct 2007, about $8.8M domestic), *Mr. Magorium's Wonder Emporium* and *Nim's Island* ([The Numbers](https://www.the-numbers.com/movie/Seeker-The-Dark-is-Rising-The-(2007)); [Christianity Today](https://www.christianitytoday.com/2006/10/walden)). Around **Oct 2–5, 2008**, about a week before Ember opened, Walden and Fox announced they were "restructuring and streamlining" Fox Walden into an in-house Fox unit. Its chief, Jeffrey Godsick, moved back to Fox marketing, and Variety reported about **a dozen layoffs** ([Dark Horizons, Oct 5, 2008](https://www.darkhorizons.com/?p=59669); [Screen Daily snippet](https://www.screendaily.com/fox-walden-restructure-sees-godsick-move-back-into-fox/4041228.article)). Because the restructuring came before the opening, it was not a reaction to Ember's failure. It was a reaction to the earlier slate. It did mean Ember was being marketed by an organization that was being dismantled, which fits Poland's "no ad budget" remark.

**International** rights followed a different route. Summit Entertainment's international sales arm presented the film to foreign buyers at Cannes in May 2007 and sold it territory by territory. In the UK, for example, Entertainment Film Distributors "swooped on" the rights ([Screen Daily snippets, 2007](https://www.screendaily.com/summit-back-at-cannes-with-walden-polanski-step-up-2/4032107.article); [IMDb company credits](https://www.imdb.de/title/tt0970411/companycredits)). This matters for the money, discussed below. In a pre-sale, the foreign distributor pays an advance up front and takes the box-office risk in its own country.

What happened afterward shows the damage. Granat left Walden effective Dec 1, 2008 ([Hollywood Reporter snippet](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/business/business-news/walden-original-has-folded-his-123072/)). Disney dropped the third Narnia film that December ([Christianity Today](https://www.christianitytoday.com/2008/12/disney-dumps-narnia/)). *The People of Sparks* was never made. At the October 2008 junket, Ronan relayed Kenan's line to the cast: "Get all of your friends to go and see it and then we'll see" ([io9, Oct 10, 2008](https://gizmodo.com/will-there-be-a-city-of-ember-2-5061434)). The claim that the sequel was then "shelved" is retrospective, and I found no 2008–09 cancellation notice ([Wikipedia: The People of Sparks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_People_of_Sparks)).

## How a film gets from a Belfast shipyard to 2,022 screens, and why Ember vanished from them

**Booking.** A distributor such as Fox does not sell a film to "theaters" in general. It licenses each film to each theater separately. Where several cinemas compete in one area, the distributor divides the country into **"film zones"** and offers a new release to one theater per zone ([Cinemark 10-K FY2008](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1385280/000095013409005288/d66662e10vk.htm)). How long a film stays is "negotiated... on a case-by-case basis." Films "with more uncertain performance" get shorter commitments ([Regal 10-K FY2008](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1168696/000104746909002095/a2191042z10-k.htm)). Ember's 2,022 theaters made it a mid-sized wide release. It had the fewest theaters of the four wide films opening that weekend; *Body of Lies* had 2,710 and *The Express* 2,808 ([BOM weekend 41](https://www.boxofficemojo.com/weekend/2008W41/)).

**Prints.** In 2008 nearly every US screen still ran 35mm film. Theater chains were only beginning to plan digital conversion ([Regal 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1168696/000104746909002095/a2191042z10-k.htm)). Each theater needed a physical print. Estimates put the cost at roughly **$1,000–$1,500 per print** ([Smith & Telang, CMU, 2016](https://www.cmu.edu/entertainment-analytics/documents/effectiveness-of-anti-piracy-efforts/windows-of-opportunity.pdf); [NPR/Epstein, 2010](https://www.npr.org/2010/04/01/124535538/crunching-numbers-in-the-hollywood-economy)). By my calculation, Ember's US prints alone would have cost about $2–3 million.

**The rental split.** Ticket money is divided between theater and distributor under a **sliding scale**, **firm terms**, or an after-the-run **"settlement."** The distributor's percentage is highest in a film's first weeks and falls over the run ([Regal 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1168696/000104746909002095/a2191042z10-k.htm); [Cinemark 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1385280/000095013409005288/d66662e10vk.htm)). In 2008 the two largest public chains paid out **52.6% (Regal) and 55.6% (Cinemark US)** of admissions as "film rental and advertising" costs. Pure rent is slightly lower than those figures because they include some theater advertising. Theaters also keep almost all concession revenue. Foreign rental rates are "generally lower than in the U.S. markets" ([Cinemark 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1385280/000095013409005288/d66662e10vk.htm)), and a common rule of thumb is about 40%, though that rule comes from later and weaker sources ([Substack explainer](https://brandstofans.substack.com/p/blockbuster-movie-economics-101-and?open=false)).

**Marketing (P&A).** "Film distributors organize and finance multimedia advertising campaigns" ([Regal 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1168696/000104746909002095/a2191042z10-k.htm)). That spending comes on top of the production budget. The last published industry average was **$35.9 million of marketing per major-studio film in 2007**, against an average production cost of $70.8 million ([Studio Daily, Mar 2008](https://studiodaily.com/2008/03/movie-budgets-on-the-up-and-up)). The MPAA stopped publishing these averages the following year ([ArtsJournal](https://www.artsjournal.org/?p=44812)). Ember's spend was never reported. Both pre-release and post-release 2008 commentary describe it as thin.

**Weekend reporting.** Studios release Sunday *estimates* that project Sunday's sales from Saturday's, then publish Monday *actuals* ([FindThisMovie](https://findthismovie.com/how-box-office-numbers-are-actually-reported/), a weak current-era source). That is why 2008 wire stories said "#10, $3.2 million" and the databases say "#11, $3.13 million."

The key measures are per-theater average and week-to-week drop:

- **Opening per-theater average.** Ember's $1,547 came to about **215 tickets per theater across the whole weekend**, by my calculation at the 2008 average ticket price of $7.18 ([The Numbers 2008 market summary](https://oak1.the-numbers.com/market/2008/summary)).
- **Total tickets.** The film's entire domestic gross works out to roughly 1.1 million tickets.
- **Theater collapse.** Weekend two fell a fairly ordinary 45%. Then theaters used their short-commitment flexibility. Ember went **from 2,023 screens to 536**, and the film was gone from the top 20 by weekend three.

## Staggered abroad and on DVD in 102 days

A 35mm-era film normally opened in the US and then rolled out abroad over months. This saved on prints, which could be refurbished for "a few hundred dollars" and shipped overseas. It also gave time for dubbing, censorship, local holidays, and foreign exhibitors who wanted to see US results first ([Smith & Telang, CMU](https://www.cmu.edu/entertainment-analytics/documents/effectiveness-of-anti-piracy-efforts/windows-of-opportunity.pdf)). Ember followed a hybrid pattern:

- **Day-and-date UK opening.** It opened in the UK on the same day as the US, Oct 10, 2008. The UK became its biggest foreign market at **$2.47 million**, about a quarter of the foreign total ([BOM](https://www.boxofficemojo.com/title/tt0970411/)).
- **Spread-out rollout elsewhere.** It reached Italy and France in December, Spain on New Year's Day, Belgium and the Netherlands in February 2009, and Portugal in **July 2009**.
- **Western Europe dominated.** The UK, Italy, Spain and France together supplied about **64% of the foreign gross**.
- **Missing territories.** Germany, Sweden, Japan and Brazil do not appear in the theatrical records. According to a retrospective, unsourced claim, the film went straight to video there ([Bomb Report](https://bombreport.com/yearly-breakdowns/2008-2/city-of-ember/)).

In that era the theatrical run largely served as advertising for **home video**, which in the mid-2000s supplied the majority of studio film revenue ([Epstein, Slate, 2005](https://slate.com/culture/2005/07/hollywood-s-death-spiral.html)). Theater chains themselves noted that DVD and pay-TV deal values "frequently depend" on theatrical success ([Regal 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1168696/000104746909002095/a2191042z10-k.htm)).

The usual gap between theaters and DVD was about four months: NATO measured **4 months 11 days** for 2009 releases, and Fox averaged 3 months 29 days ([Knowledge@Wharton, Nov 2009](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/reel-time-the-incredible-shrinking-window-for-movie-releases/)). Ember's DVD arrived on **Jan 20, 2009, 102 days after opening**. That was faster than Fox's own average, and only nine days after the last weekend its theatrical run was tracked ([The Numbers](https://www.the-numbers.com/movie/City-of-Ember)). There was no US Blu-ray, and Bomb Report (retrospective) says Fox removed all extras from the DVD ([Bomb Report](https://bombreport.com/yearly-breakdowns/2008-2/city-of-ember/)). The disc also landed in a shrinking market: US home-video spending had peaked at $24.1 billion in 2006 and fell to $22.4 billion in 2008 ([Knowledge@Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/reel-time-the-incredible-shrinking-window-for-movie-releases/)). DVD earned roughly what the theaters did, about $8.4 million in consumer spending, which was respectable but nowhere near enough.

## Running the numbers: a loss of roughly $25–65 million

The real accounts are confidential, so the estimate below uses explicit assumptions. It treats all funders together (Walden, Playtone, Fox and the foreign buyers) as one pool of money. The rental and window rates are drawn from the sources above. My own assumptions are labeled as such.

| Line item | Low-loss case | Central | High-loss case | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Domestic rentals (share of $7.87M gross) | 55% → $4.3M | 53% → $4.2M | 50% → $3.9M | Regal/Cinemark 2008 rates; a front-loaded run keeps rates high. Bomb Report's estimate is also ~$4.2M |
| International rentals (share of ~$10.06M) | 50% → $5.0M | 40% → $4.0M | 35% → $3.5M | Lower foreign rates; this pot first has to cover local distributors' own costs |
| US DVD net (share of $8.38M consumer spend) | 60% → $5.0M | 50% → $4.2M | 40% → $3.4M | **My assumption** (retail margin, manufacturing, returns); no sourced rate |
| TV, foreign video, other | $10M | $6M | $3M | **My assumption**; no data found |
| N. Ireland Screen grant | ~$1.6M | ~$1.6M | ~$1.6M | £800K, converted at ~2007 rates |
| **Total money back** | **~$26M** | **~$20M** | **~$15M** | |
| Production budget | $38M | $38M / $55M | $55M | The two reported figures |
| Domestic P&A | $12M | $18M | $25M | **My assumption**: ~$2–3M prints plus a "forceless" ad push well below the $35.9M studio average |
| **Net result** | **≈ −$24M** | **≈ −$36M / −$53M** | **≈ −$65M** | |

As a cross-check, the common rule of thumb says a film needs **2–2.5× its budget in worldwide gross** to break even in theaters. That rule comes from a weak source and ignores home video ([GeekVibesNation](https://geekvibesnation.com/how-much-does-a-movie-need-to-make-to-be-profitable/)). For Ember it implies $76–138 million. The film reached **13–24% of that threshold**.

The losses were not shared evenly. Foreign distributors that paid Summit advance guarantees in 2007 would have absorbed much of the overseas shortfall: about $4 million of foreign rentals spread across some 30 territories, before their own advertising, cannot have recouped much. That probably cushioned Walden and Playtone. In a typical studio arrangement, the distributor also recovers its fee and P&A from the first dollars in. If Fox-Walden followed that pattern, the producing financiers bore most of the loss. The actual terms were never disclosed, so the allocation is an inference. The overall conclusion is not: on any reasonable assumptions, City of Ember lost tens of millions of dollars.

## Conclusion

Ember's failure is mainly a case of distribution failing, not of a bad film. It reached audiences who rated it B+ and critics who leaned positive. It did so in a crowded October weekend, at the lowest per-screen level of nearly any 2008 wide release, through a marketing label shut down the week before opening. The theater system's short, performance-based licenses then removed it within three weeks. In the 35mm era a soft opening was close to final, because few screens meant little awareness and so little afterlife. The early DVD release with stripped extras suggests the studio had already written the film off.

There is also a lesson about sources. The firmest numbers, the grosses, survive only in databases, and the most quotable 2008 verdicts (Deadline, Variety, the LA Times) sit behind paywalls or archive blocks. The one contemporaneous budget figure, $38 million, has been displaced in popular memory by the later $55 million. Anyone citing Ember's "$55 million flop" should know that the period record supports "flop" firmly, while the size of the budget remains disputed.
